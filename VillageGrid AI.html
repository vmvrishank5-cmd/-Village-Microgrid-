Method and assumptions

Every 15 minutes: solar first serves load directly. Surplus charges the battery (95% efficient, up to 20 kW, never above capacity). A deficit is met by the battery (95% efficient, 20 kW, never below the 10% reserve), then grid (if connected, 15 kW), then simulated diesel (if on, 5 kW). Anything left is unmet demand. The battery never charges and discharges in the same step. Energy (kWh) = power (kW) × 0.25 h.

Assumptions: solar output = capacity × 0.85 × sin-shaped daylight curve (06:00 to 18:00) × cloud factor (1 − 0.75 × cloud). Loads: 50 homes (~2 kWh/day each), school 2.5 kW (08:00 to 15:00), clinic 0.6 kW (+0.8 kW daytime), pump 1.5 kW (06:00 to 09:00 and 17:00 to 19:00), streetlights 0.6 kW at night. CO₂ = renewable kWh used × baseline − solar kWh generated × 0.045 kg/kWh. Emission factors are educational assumptions to verify before real-world use.

Competition demonstration

Problem: villages near tier-2 cities lose 4–6 hours of power a day. Solution: solar + battery microgrid with priority for the clinic and lights. Impact: fewer outages, less diesel, lower CO₂.

About VillageGrid AI

Problem: Rampur Kheda (38 km from Nashik) loses power 4–6 hours a day, hurting homes, school, clinic and water supply. Proposal: a solar microgrid with battery storage and fair priority rules for 50 households.

Limitations: it is a simulation with assumed values; it has no real weather, grid or sensor data and does not control hardware.

Benefits: more reliable essential services, less diesel use, lower emissions, and a tool for learning about renewable energy.
